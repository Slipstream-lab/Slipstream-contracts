# Pattern 06 - Event Log

## Contention problem

The **naive** contract is an append-only log. Every `append()` reads the single
shared `Tail` pointer, writes the new entry under `Entry(tail)`, and bumps
`Tail`. Because **every append from every writer** reads and writes that one
persistent key, all appends conflict on it under CAP-0063 and serialise -- a
global bottleneck imposed on otherwise independent log writes.

The write-footprint of a naive append is `{Tail, Entry(tail)}`: one shared
hot key (`Tail`, a `read-modify-write`) plus a dynamic entry key.

## The optimization

The **optimized** contract splits the log into `SEGMENTS` (8) independent
per-writer segments, each with its own `SegmentTail(segment)` pointer and
entries under `Entry(segment, index)`:

- `append(segment, msg)` writes only `{SegmentTail(segment), Entry(segment, tail)}`.
- Appends to **different** segments have disjoint footprints and do not conflict.
- Appends within one segment still serialize, which is correct: they are an
  ordered per-writer stream.
- The read side (`segment_len`, `total_len`) aggregates across segments at the
  price of touching several tail keys -- the rare reporting cost.

## Why it improves parallelism (CAP-0063)

- Naive: all `M` appends from all writers write the one `Tail` key ->
  `M`-clique -> ~`M` stages (fully serial).
- Optimized: appends to distinct segments share a stage. With `S` segments,
  `M` appends spread evenly across them need roughly `ceil(M / S)` stages.
- `append`'s write-footprint drops from `{Tail, Entry(tail)}` to
  `{SegmentTail(segment), Entry(segment, tail)}` -- the shared key is gone.

A caveat to be honest about: `slipstream-core` cannot resolve
`SegmentTail(segment)` at compile time, so it still reports a
`read-modify-write` on a `(dynamic)` key for the optimized `append`. The real
win is structural -- the tail is now per-segment and per-writer, not global --
and shows up in the key-shape / conflict-graph analysis rather than in the raw
detector count.

## Benchmark methodology (via slipstream-core)

1. `slipstream scan` both variants; capture the footprint of `append` and the
   `read-modify-write` detector findings.
2. `slipstream diff naive optimized --json`; confirm `append`'s write-footprint
   drops from `{Tail, Entry(tail)}` to `{SegmentTail(segment), Entry(...)}`
   (one fewer storage write).
3. Synthetic batch of `M` appends across `S` segments; have slipstream-core
   compute conflict-graph edges and stages.
4. Report edges/stages (expect naive ~`M` stages, optimized ~`ceil(M / S)`),
   and note the key-shape change (shared global tail -> per-writer segment tail)
   as the qualitative win the raw detector count understates.
